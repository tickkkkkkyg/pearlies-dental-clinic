Why a check-up every six months is not a scam

Small problems are cheap. Big problems are not. The six-month visit is mostly about catching the small ones.

There is a common suspicion that six-month check-ups exist to keep dentists busy. They do not. They exist because tooth decay and gum disease are both slow and silent, and the gap between "fine" and "expensive" is usually measured in months, not years.

A small cavity caught early is a twenty-minute filling. The same cavity ignored for a year can reach the nerve, and then it is a root canal and a crown — ten times the cost and ten times the time in the chair. The check-up is the cheap part.

Gum disease is worse, because it does not hurt at all until it is advanced. By the time a tooth feels loose, the bone supporting it has already gone. A six-month clean and probe catches the inflammation while it is still reversible.

So no, the six-month visit is not a scam. It is the cheapest insurance you can buy for your teeth, and it is the one appointment most people skip.
